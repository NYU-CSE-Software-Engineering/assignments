# Project Presentation Assessment Rubric
## Introduction
The presentation will be scored based on the rubric items show below. The rubric scoring range is between 0 and 35 points.

## Section I: Content & Structure (Adherence to Guidelines) (0-5 points)
| Criterion | Exemplary (5) | Satisfactory (4-3) | Developing (2-1) | Unsatisfactory (0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Introduction & Overview** | All required elements (Team, App Name, Member Roles/Contributions) are clearly and engagingly presented. The Project Overview acts as a compelling "elevator pitch". | All required elements for the Introduction and Project Overview are present, addressing the problem solved and why the project matters. | One or two required elements are missing (e.g., specific member contributions or the problem statement), or the pitch fails to engage the audience. | Core information (Team/App name, Project Goal) is missing, or the time allocation is significantly off.
| **Key Functionality Overview** | Key/main functionality is insightfully discussed, with clear, concise reasoning for its inclusion and a mention of planned/cut features. | Key/main functionality is summarized, and the importance of each part is adequately explained. | Summary of functionality is vague or incomplete, or the discussion of why features were included is missing. | Key functionality is not clearly summarized, or time is not respected. |

## Section II: Architecture and Data Flow (Technical Depth) (0-5 points)
| Criterion | Exemplary (5) | Satisfactory (4-3) | Developing (2-1) | Unsatisfactory (0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **REST/Non-REST Endpoints** | A concise list of all primary RESTful resources/actions is provided, and all non-RESTful endpoints are clearly justified by specific business requirements. | Primary RESTful resources and supported actions are mapped correctly, and any custom non-RESTful routes are identified and justified. | Mapping of RESTful resources is incomplete or confusing, and/or non-RESTful routes are used without adequate justification. | Architecture is not discussed, or the RESTful/non-RESTful mapping is fundamentally incorrect. |
| **Models, Views, and Controllers (MVC)** | Roles of MVC are expertly explained, and the most important Models are detailed, including complex associations and key business logic (scopes/validations). | The flow and roles of MVC are clearly explained, and the two to three most important Models are discussed, detailing their associations and custom logic. | Explanation of the MVC flow is superficial, or the discussion of Model associations and business logic is limited or incorrect. | No clear explanation of the MVC flow, or important model depth is omitted. |
| **Database Schema** | A simplified, highly readable diagram is included and used to explain indexing, complex data types, or polymorphic association design decisions. | A simplified, readable diagram of core tables and relationships is included and discussed. | A diagram is included but is difficult to read from the back, or design decisions (indexing, complex types) are not explained. | No database schema diagram is included in the presentation. |

## Section III: Implementation & Production Readiness (0-5 points)
| Criterion | Exemplary (5) | Satisfactory (4-3) | Developing (2-1) | Unsatisfactory (0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Key Gems and External Services** | The three most critical non-standard gems are discussed, including their purpose, high impact, and clear examples of how they saved time or enabled key features. | Three critical non-standard gems are discussed, covering their purpose and general impact on the project. | Three critical non-standard gems are discussed, covering their purpose and general impact on the project. | This section is omitted, or only default Rails gems are discussed. |
| **Testing, QA, and Deployment** | Team's testing strategy (types of tests, coverage goals) and the deployment pipeline are detailed, highlighting key challenges/differences between dev and production environments. | Team's testing strategy (types of tests) and deployment process (hosting platform) are clearly described. | Description of testing is superficial, or the deployment process is only briefly mentioned without addressing production challenges. | No discussion of testing strategy or deployment pipeline. |

## Section IV: Demo of Key Functionality (0-5 points)
| Criterion | Exemplary (5) | Satisfactory (4-3) | Developing (2-1) | Unsatisfactory (0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | The live demo is smooth, highly efficient, and flawlessly showcases the biggest and most important features and the core value proposition of the SaaS application. | The live demo is functional, focuses on key features, and walks the audience through the main user workflows. | Demo is disorganized, runs over time, includes unnecessary features (like sign-up), or encounters multiple minor technical errors. | The demo is non-functional, or core features cannot be successfully demonstrated. |

## Section V: Concluding Remarks & Reflection (0-5 points)
| Criterion | Exemplary (5) | Satisfactory (4-3) | Developing (2-1) | Unsatisfactory (0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Concluding Remarks & Reflection** | A single, high-impact item is provided for each of the three required areas (Success, Challenge, Lesson Learned) and demonstrates deep, non-generic insight into the development process. | A clear, distinct item is provided for each of the three required areas (Success, Challenge, Lesson Learned). | One or two of the three required reflection items are missing, or the answers are generic (e.g., "We communicated well"). | The Concluding Remarks section is rushed, incomplete, or entirely missing. |

## Section VI: Q&A Engagement (0-5 points)
| Criterion | Exemplary (5) | Satisfactory (4-3) | Developing (2-1) | Unsatisfactory (0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Q&A Engagement** | The team is highly prepared, confidently and effectively answering all questions, often preemptively assigning experts to handle specific domains. | The team answers questions clearly and maintains a professional demeanor, demonstrating a comprehensive understanding of the project. | Answers are hesitant, vague, or one team member dominates the Q&A section, or the team fails to embrace critical analysis professionally. | The team cannot answer basic questions about their design choices or technology stack. |

## Section VII: Professionalism & Presentation Delivery (0-5 points)
| Criterion | Exemplary (5) | Satisfactory (4-3) | Developing (2-1) | Unsatisfactory (0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Clarity, Pacing, and Timing** | The presentation is smooth, clear, and easy to understand from the back of the room. The team adheres precisely to all time limits. | The presentation is clear and generally well-paced. The team adheres to the total 45-minute limit (excluding the Q&A time), with minor deviations in individual section timing. | Delivery is inconsistent (e.g., reading from cards, mumbling), or the presentation significantly runs over the time limits. | Presentation is completely disorganized, incomprehensible, or cut off due to excessive length. |
| **Team Participation & Presence** | All team members are present, on time, present for roughly equal time, and maintain professional conduct throughout. | All team members are present and present for an adequate, though not perfectly equal, amount of time. | One team member presents for a disproportionately small amount of time, or non-speaking members block the screen. | A team member is absent, or professional conduct is not maintained. |
